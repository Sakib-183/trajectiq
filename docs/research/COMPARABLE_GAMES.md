# Reference review — design decisions for TRAJECTIQ 3

Reviewed the official descriptions, not a hands-on comparative performance test. No claim that TRAJECTIQ is universally better or more likely to win.

## NASA Space Voyagers

Source: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/lesson-plan/nasa-space-voyagers-the-game/

The official lesson describes a resource-based spacecraft/instrument card game with destination challenges and research points. Useful lesson: a beginner needs clear categories, a visible objective and a short explanation of what a choice changes.

TRAJECTIQ response: progressive coach, seven readable subsystem groups, a 520-unit success threshold, and visible mass/power/data/cost consequences. It adds time-evolving resource accounting and a causal redesign comparison; it does not reproduce the cards or imply affiliation.

## Moonbase Alpha

Source: https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Spinoff2011/cg_4.html

The official description focuses on restoring lunar life support with limited time/resources and coordinated player roles. Useful lesson: resource management feels like a game when decisions are tied to an immediate mission problem.

TRAJECTIQ response: events pause for deliberate recovery choices, consequences are visible before resuming, the orbit timeline explains upcoming shadow/contact windows, and the assistant suggests six local team roles. This version does not implement network multiplayer or a surface repair game.

## Scope

TRAJECTIQ focuses on the complete design → launch → operation → consequence → redesign learning loop, free offline operation, traceable NASA environmental data and an honest in-game assistant. These choices are improvements against our previous version, not evidence of superiority over all submissions.
